# Working principles

Apply Interchange when coordination earns its cost: delegated implementation, independent review, multi-repository dependencies, a resumable investigation or cross-session handover. For a small self-contained action, do it directly under the repository's rules; do not create workers, packets and a milestone merely to use the skill. An independent review requirement still applies where adopted.

- **Smallest sound solution.** Reuse a supported path and existing patterns. Add abstraction, framework, state or automation for a present requirement, demonstrated repeated need, or concrete predicted development in the accepted roadmap. For anticipatory design, name the future consumer/use case, the rework avoided, and why the incremental cost now is proportionate; keep future behavior out of this slice unless authorized. A speculative possibility alone is insufficient. File count is a scope signal, not a success metric.
- **Fix the cause.** Diagnose before repair. Never hide a failing invariant with a bypass, silent fallback, disabled check or fabricated success. A supported alternative is acceptable when its trade-off is explicit and it still meets acceptance. Emergency containment is named, authorized, reversible and tracked to resolution.
- **Concrete language.** Say what changed, what evidence proves it, what remains and who acts next. Omit praise, filler, repetitive ceremony and confidence unsupported by evidence. Unknown is a valid result.
- **One authority per fact.** Scope/decisions in the plan, live delivery in GitHub, proof in artifacts. Reports and diagrams summarize and link; they do not become competing specifications.
- **Decide once, reuse deliberately.** Resolve material behavior before dispatch, carry accepted decisions into the packet, allow routine implementation judgment, and ask only about a real gap. Configurability is a product choice when supported by the accepted plan, not permission to build an unnecessary settings framework.
- **Proportionate proof.** Prove the user outcome and meaningful failure paths. Keep independent acceptance; rerun only what change/risk/repository policy warrants. Neither fewer tests nor more tests is the objective.
- **Finish the bounded outcome.** Preserve unrelated work, ticket unrelated discoveries, return truthful gates, and reconcile delivery. A worker exit, green test or merge is not interchangeable with accepted product behavior.
- **Earn complexity.** Before adding a rule or artifact, name the failure it prevents and its owner. Prefer editing an existing rule; remove superseded material from active routing. If it creates more coordination than it saves, simplify it.

## Reassess safe parallelism; reuse useful context

The initial worker count is not a permanent limit or a utilization target. At plan approval and whenever a worker returns, a blocker clears, ownership is released or scope changes, the Planner reassesses the approved ready work. Start or resume as many independent assignments as are safe and supported by the actual platform capacity; do not keep two simply because the plan began with two, or continue with one merely because the other finished.

On every return, inspect the whole approved dependency graph, not only that worker's next task: one completion may release several independent jobs. Reconcile the return and its actual acceptance/ownership gates first, identify all newly ready jobs, then dispatch/resume the safe authorized set or record why each remains held. A worker-complete claim alone does not satisfy an independent-review or merge dependency.

Before dispatch, check dependencies, overlapping write surfaces, shared runtime/test resources, review capacity and the configured model/cost authority. More workers are useful only when they advance coherent accepted deliverables without competing ownership or disproportionate coordination. Serialize conflicting steps while allowing independent work around them. Platform concurrency limits remain hard constraints; do not evade them through another client. A user hold or “no next slice” boundary remains binding: reassessment may identify opportunities, but cannot authorize new scope.

Prefer resuming an existing idle agent for related work when its retained context is relevant and its model, role and ownership remain appropriate. Give it a new assignment/attempt record, the current baseline and changed decisions; stale context never overrides the new packet. Use a fresh agent for unrelated work, role changes, unreliable/stale context, or when independence requires it. In particular, a writer must not become its own independent reviewer. Do not leave an otherwise suitable agent idle through habit, and do not reuse it merely to avoid a clean handover.

Record one compact scheduling decision in the coordination handover at these events: ready work; safe parallel opportunities; agent reused/new; or why no additional work is released (dependency, resource, capacity, authority, or no ready work). Reuse that record in the next status report. This is event-driven scheduling, not polling, mandatory worker occupancy or a new approval gate.
